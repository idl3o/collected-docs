# Ethics (Virtue, Deontology, Utilitarianism)

**Summary:**
Ethics navigates the complexities of moral decision-making by examining three primary frameworks – Virtue Ethics, Deontology, and Utilitarianism – to arrive at nuanced conclusions about right and wrong actions rooted in philosophical inquiry, practical wisdom, and empathy.

**Key Frameworks:**
- Virtue Ethics (Aristotle's Nicomachean Ethics)
- Care-based Virtue Ethics (Gilligan; In a Different Voice)
- Immanuel Kant's Deontology
- Jeremy Bentham's Utilitarianism

**Actionable Insights:**
- Deontology's rule-based ethics can lead to difficulties in reconciling contradictory duties, potentially neglecting contextual factors; Virtue Ethics emphasizes the cultivation of moral character through self-reflection and personal growth, risking disregard for situational complexities; Utilitarianism prioritizes outcomes over individual rights, risking disregard for intrinsic value and diversity of moral perspectives
